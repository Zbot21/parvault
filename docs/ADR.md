## 1.1 Active `v0.4.0` Cryptographic Suite & Classical-to-Post-Quantum Evolution Guide

A recurring question when reading historical `v0.1` ADRs ([`ADR-01`](adr/ADR-01-streaming-stripes-and-encrypt-before-shard.md), [`ADR-02`](adr/ADR-02-dht-encrypted-manifests.md), [`ADR-05`](adr/ADR-05-recursive-wot-and-subtree-slashing.md), [`ADR-09`](adr/ADR-09-byzantine-merkle-quarantine-and-egress-slashing.md)) alongside the post-quantum ADRs ([`ADR-12`](adr/ADR-12-asymmetric-post-quantum-hybrids.md) and [`ADR-13`](adr/ADR-13-symmetric-pq-grover-baseline-and-cascaded-aead.md)) is **why symmetric `AES-256-GCM` and Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) coexist in `v0.4.0`**:

1. **Asymmetric Public-Key Primitives vs. Shor's Algorithm ([`ADR-12`](adr/ADR-12-asymmetric-post-quantum-hybrids.md)):**
   - On a future Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC), **Shor's algorithm** solves elliptic-curve discrete logarithms in polynomial time—completely breaking standalone `X25519` key agreement and forging standalone `Ed25519` signatures.
   - Therefore, every long-lived asymmetric boundary exposed to **Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later (HNDL)** recording or long-term identity verification upgrades to **mandatory classical + NIST post-quantum lattice hybrids**: X-Wing style **`X25519 + ML-KEM-768` (`NIST FIPS 203`)** via `HKDF-SHA512` (`HybridKemKeypair`) and composite **`Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65` (`NIST FIPS 204`)** dual verification (`HybridPqSigningKeypair`).
2. **Symmetric Ciphers & Hash PRFs vs. Grover's Algorithm ([`ADR-13`](adr/ADR-13-symmetric-pq-grover-baseline-and-cascaded-aead.md)):**
   - Unstructured symmetric block/stream ciphers and keyed hash PRFs are **not** vulnerable to Shor's algorithm; they face only **Grover's quantum search**, which yields at most a quadratic $\mathcal{O}(2^{n/2})$ speedup.
   - Consequently, **256-bit `AES-256-GCM` and 256-bit `HMAC-SHA256` already provide 128-bit post-quantum security (NIST Post-Quantum Category 5)**—the highest NIST PQ security floor—while streaming at `496.5–589.2 MiB/s` inside `< 10 MiB` RAM on embedded Router-SoCs.
3. **Why `PqVaultMasterKey` Cascades `AES-256-GCM` + `ChaCha20-Poly1305` ([`ADR-13`](adr/ADR-13-symmetric-pq-grover-baseline-and-cascaded-aead.md)):**
   - `PqVaultMasterKey` encrypts first with 256-bit `AES-256-GCM` ($K_1$) and then wraps the output in 256-bit `ChaCha20-Poly1305` ($K_2$) for **algebraic cipher-diversity defense-in-depth** (`S-box` SPN + $GF(2^{128})$ GHASH vs. ARX stream cipher + prime-field Poly1305), **not** because `AES-256-GCM` is broken by quantum computers. Both layers independently achieve the 128-bit PQ Grover floor, and both would have to fail simultaneously to expose root vault superblocks (`EncryptedVaultCatalogEnvelope`) or cascaded vault stripes.
4. **Point-to-Point Wire Transport (`Noise XX`) vs. End-to-End HNDL Payload Confidentiality ([`ADR-08`](adr/ADR-08-noise-xx-transport-and-bounded-wire-framing.md)):**
   - `libp2p` point-to-point TCP connections use `Noise_XX_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256` (`Yamux`) strictly for live peer authentication, `/p2p/<PeerId>` pinning, and outer RPC framing. End-to-end HNDL confidentiality **never** depends on the outer transport session because every file stripe, manifest, and 2-layer `PqRelayedShardEnvelope` is encrypted client-side (`AES-256-GCM` / `PqVaultMasterKey`) and/or encapsulated under `X25519 + ML-KEM-768` *before* touching the wire.
